It works

This is the built-in site of the luci-app-xtunnel package, served while default is the selected site. The router already serves it over HTTPS with a real certificate; these pages explain how to make a site of your own: what goes into the archive, how to have the router rebuild the pages on a schedule, and how to replace the standard login of the router administration with a login page of your own.

How it works

A site is a .tar.gz archive. You upload it on the XTunnel → Dynamic DNS → Web site page of LuCI, pick it in the list together with the domain it answers to, and click Save & Apply. From then on the router's web server (uhttpd) serves the files of the archive instead of its administration pages, and the HTTPS port is opened from the Internet.

An archive may hold three kinds of content:

WhatWhere in the archiveRequiredWhat the router does with it
Public filesthe top level of the archive (or a single folder)index.html — yesserves them as they are over HTTPS
Build script.xtunnel-ddns/build and whatever it needs next to itnokeeps it away from the web server, runs it after the upload, on the schedule and on the button
Login pagecgi-bin/loginnomakes it the login of the router administration instead of the standard LuCI page

A minimal site in a minute

mkdir mysite
cat > mysite/index.html <<'EOF'
<!doctype html><meta charset="utf-8"><title>My site</title>
<h1>Hello</h1>
EOF
tar -C mysite -czf mysite.tar.gz .

Upload mysite.tar.gz with Upload a site archive…, choose it in the Site field and a domain in the Domain field, tick Serve the web site and click Save & Apply. The site opens at https://your-domain/.

What the router can do

  • Static files of any size within the free space (shown in LuCI next to the list of sites). The sysupgrade backup is built in RAM, so tens of megabytes are a bad idea.
  • Pages built by your own script: shell and ucode (a JavaScript-like language present on every OpenWrt with LuCI), downloads with wget. There is no Python, Node or Perl on the router, and installing them is not worth it: the router is small.
  • A login page of your own, with a captcha generated on the router and growing delays after failed attempts.
  • A Let's Encrypt certificate for the chosen domain, renewed automatically.

The administration stays reachable at /cgi-bin/luci/ of the same site. To keep it working, do not name your files or folders cgi-bin (apart from your own CGI scripts inside it) or luci-static.
